## Top 3 risks

1. Public harm - there is a high likelihood of such computers coming across errors or them being hacked, and given how much the public uses cars, there is an even greater chance of many accidents happening were humans get hurt. 
2. Job loss - professional drivers would be out of work, loss of a strong workforce.
3. Loss of general competency - people will stop learning how to drive.

## Mitigating Risks
1. Conducting extensive tests on the driving computers, having a 100% success rate, enforcing strong security measures to prevent malicious users from hacking into the cars.
2. Only allow such cars to operate in specific, well-studied, low risk areas, and leave complicated work to professional drivers (this would address risks 2 and 3).

3 - If not 100%, then there should be a strategy to mitigate risk - preventative. Who will be responsibly for this and also accountability - business, software developer, etc. Point 2 is good since its good to test things in a relatively isolated, controlled environment. Seems good.

## My recommendation

No. Given the great likelihood and how damaging the risks listed above are - especially public harm - it is unrealistic to assume that a 0% accident rate can be achieved in any time frame, and because of everybody relying on transport, especially in the Greater Sydney region, it is not a good idea to suddenly allow driverless cars to drive everybody around. The laws should be changed to allow the use of such cars in well-tested, low pedestrian traffic areas, that require the least technical ability possible.
