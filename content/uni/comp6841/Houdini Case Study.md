Spiritualism is the belief that the spirits of the dead have both the ability and the inclination to communicate with the living - 1840s to1920s, peaked 1897 with 8 million followers+ in US and Europe. Arthur Connan Doyle was himself a spiritualist, despite his very rational and deductive detective stories “Sherlock Holmes”. 

Mediums are people who claim to speak with the spirits of dead people. Mina “Margery” Stinson Crandon was one of the most famous examples of a medium. 

Many people were grieving from deceased family and friends, especially after the American Civil War (1861), Spanish-American War (1898), Spanish flu (1918), WW1 (1914). So, throughout the Spiritualist movement, there were many people probably looking for a way to reconnect with deceased loved ones – it isn’t hard to see how mediums would be popular. 

Mina “margery” stinson crandon (1888-1941) 

Born in 1888, moved to Boston at a young age. Married to Dr. Le Roi Goddard Crandon, a highly respected instructor at Harvard Medical School. Became a medium in 1923 after Crandon became extremely interested in psychical research.  

- Despite her results being amazing, medium sessions were only by invitation. 
    

- Prof. William McDougall, head of Harvard’s Department of Psychology, after 5 months of observing, said her performance was fraudulent. 
    

- In 1923 she consented to being tested by a group of scientists and critics (including Harry Houdini) to see if she really had powers as a medium, the Scientific American offered $2,500 to anyone who could prove the psychic powers. She underwent an exhaustive list of experiments, some painful, and although it is thought that in some experiments, she might’ve been fraudulent, there were notable experiments where she did show psychic powers. Houdini tried very hard to expose her, but wasn’t able to in the end. 
    

- 1925, J. Malcom Bird published a book that summarised the investigation, saying there wasn’t a conclusion to say Margery’s powers were real or not. 
    

- She remained willing to perform all the tests, and didn’t seem to resent any of the investigators. 
    

- There are cases where she could’ve been fraudulent, when Margery’s ghostly brother Crandon left fingerprints, and her dentist Dr. Kerwin admitted that he gave her a waxed fingerprint, that matched exactly that of Margery’s brother, and through this and other evidence, the police demoted the status of “Walter” to a disembodied voice only. 
    

- Crandon and her husband were known for baiting investigators and trying to fool them, if possible, but many researchers today believe that some elements of the paranormal were present in Crandon’s performances. Her powers remain mysterious to this day.
