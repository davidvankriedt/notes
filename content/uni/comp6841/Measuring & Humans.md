In cybersecurity, we measure a lot of uncertain factors, and as a result we often make many assumptions.

Evidence strength - How confident are we that this is an attack?
Witness reliability - Can we trust this message or identity?
Probability of guilt - How likely is this behaviour malicious?
Risk assessment - What harm could happen?
Criminal profiling - What patterns suggest suspicious behaviour?
Fraud detection - Is this transaction genuine or fake?
Misinformation and influence - Is this content reliable or manipulated?

[[Entropy]] - how much uncertainty remains before we know the answer - [Claude Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Shannon) 1948
e.g. a guessable password has low entropy --> another way of saying it is entropy is the average number of optimal yes/no questions you have to ask to identify a value in a set.

In security, more bit means more guesses. 8 bits = 256 possibilities, 40 bits = 1 trillion possibilities. Magnitude matters more than precision.
