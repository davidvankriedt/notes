## Visibility of system status
Keep users informed about what is happening, through appropriate feedback within a reasonable time.

## Match between system & real world
Speak user's language, not the system's - use familiar concepts, language, and conventions that match how people think and talk in the real world over internal jargon or technical codes.

## User Control & Freedom
Let users undo, exit, and recover easily, so people feel in control. e.g. Ctrl+Z, being able to exit a form.

## Consistency & Standards
Follow conventions users already know.

## Error prevention
Prevent problems before they occur.

## Help users recognise, diagnose, and recover from errors
Make errors easy to understand for the average user.

## Recognition rather than recall
Minimise user's cognitive workload, users should be able to choose from options rather than remember the available options. e.g. displaying all available fonts rather than making user remember exactly what font they want.

## Flexibility & Efficiency of use
Support novices and power users alike - e.g. offering shortcuts, customisation without cluttering the system for all users.

## Help & Documentation
Provide help when users need it - even the best interfaces need searchable, task-focused help/
