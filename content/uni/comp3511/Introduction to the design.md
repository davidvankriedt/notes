__HCI__ (Human Computer Interaction) - The design, implementation and evaluation of interactive systems that considers the users at every stage of the process.

### User Experience Design (UX)
- Developing usable products - easy to learn, effective to use, enjoyable experience.
- Optimising user interaction with a system, environment or product so that they support and extend user's activities in effective, useful and usable ways.
- Involving users in the design process - establishing requirements, design, evaluating and iteration.

__Core characteristics of UX Design__:
- Users should be involved through the development of the project
- UX design focuses on what, how and why you are designing something
- Understanding users needs and their requirements and what kinds of designs should be appropriate
- Specific usability and user experience goals need to be identified, clearly documented and agreed at the beginning of the project.
- Iteration is needed through the core activities.

__UX designers vs. programmers__:
- Architect vs builder

__UX designers should have creative thinking skill__:
- Coming up with new ideas
- Thinking about alternatives
- Forming new associations
- Solving the existing problems by proposing new ideas
- Proposing ideas that can consider cognitive barriers of users.

__UX designers can help the design & development team__:
- Understand how to design interactive products that fit with what people want, need and may desire
- Appreciate that one size does not fit all (e.g. teenagers vs grown-ups)
- Identify any incorrect assumptions they may have about articular user groups (not all old people want big fonts)
- Be aware of both people's sensitivities and their capabilities
- __HUGE__ saving in time and money to get design right from the start.

__As a UX designer, one must be careful not to make wrong assumptions__:
- People would not mind wearing glasses that are needed to see in 3d in their living rooms (reasonable).
- People would not mind paying a lot more for a new 3D-enabled TV (unreasonable).
- People would really enjoy the enhanced clarity and colour detail provided by 3D (reasonable).
- People will be happy carrying around their own special glasses (reasonable to select number of users).

### [[Conceptual Models]]
- An abstract, possibly simplified idea that a person forms in their mind about an object or situation.
- A high-level description of how a system is organised and operates.
- By creating interfaces and apps that echo conceptual models, designers build on existing knowledge and frameworks.
- Making it easier for users to learn how to use the new product.

__Good vs. bad design based on conceptual models__:
- Good: 
	- Does the system convey the conceptual model of the designer to the user?
	- A good design will articulate the designer's conceptual model in the system image that enables the user to form the correct conceptual model in their own mind.
- Bad:
	- A poor design can arise from the differences between the conceptual model that the designer intended, and the conceptual model formed in the user's mind.

### Use of metaphors
- Exploit user's familiar knowledge, helping them to understand the unfamiliar.
- In UI design, an interface metaphor is a set of user interface visuals, actions or procedures that exploit specific knowledge that users already have of other domains (e.g. + icon to add something, mail logo, camera logo, scissor logo, magnifying glass logo).

__Benefits of interface metaphors__:
- Makes learning new systems easier.
- Helps users understand the underlying conceptual model.
- Can enable the realm of computers and their applications to be made more accessible to a greater diversity of users.

__Problems with interface metaphors__:
- Forces users to only understand the system in terms of the metaphor.
- Can limit designers' imagination in coming up with new conceptual models.
- Designers can inadvertently use bad existing designs and transfer the bad parts over.

### Sketching User Interfaces
- You create a mock-up as part of the design process (better quality than a sketch), to discuss issues with others and evaluate your interface with users.
- Sketches help provoke conversations among community members during a phase of the design process that does not always incorporate user perspective and reflections.
